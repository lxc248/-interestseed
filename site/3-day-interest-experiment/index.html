How to run a 3-day interest experiment

A connection is a hypothesis. Three days of evidence is enough to decide whether to keep going, change direction, or drop it.

Updated 2026-11-06 · 6 min read

What counts as a 3-day experiment

It is a small personal test, not a clinical trial. You take one saved interest, connect it to something you already care about, and do one concrete action on each of three days. After each action you write one observation: what happened, what you felt, what you avoided.

The unit is one connection, not one topic. “Learn design” is too broad. “Test whether I enjoy turning a saved UI note into a one-screen redesign” is testable.

Before day 1

  1. Pick one saved note or idea that still has some pull.
  2. Write the connection as a sentence: “If I try X, I will learn Y.”
  3. Choose an action small enough to finish in 15–30 minutes.
  4. Decide what would count as a useful observation, not a success or failure.

If you cannot name a concrete action, the connection is still too vague. Do not start with a 3-day commitment to a fog.

Day 1, 2 and 3

Day 1 — do the smallest real version

Make something, try something, or ask someone. Write one paragraph: what you did, how long it took, what surprised you.

Day 2 — repeat or notice resistance

Do a second small action. If you avoid it, write that down. Avoidance is data, not a moral failure.

Day 3 — look at the pattern

Do the third action, then read all three observations. Decide: continue, change the direction, or drop it for now.

What counts as evidence

Useful evidence is concrete and observable:

  • what you actually did, and for how long;
  • what you finished, shipped, or showed someone;
  • what felt easier or harder than expected;
  • what you avoided, delayed, or forgot;
  • a quote, a response, a result, or a changed decision.

“It was interesting” is a feeling, not evidence. “I spent 25 minutes and wanted another 25” is evidence.

Decision rules

What you seeDecision
You acted all three days and want a next stepContinue with a slightly larger test.
You acted, but the direction feels wrongChange the connection, keep the interest.
You avoided it repeatedly and have no curiosity leftDrop it for now; write why.
The action was too big to startShrink it and rerun; do not call it a failure.

Common failure modes

  • Testing a topic instead of an action. “Learn photography” is not testable; “edit five photos and show one person” is.
  • Making day 1 too large. A 3-hour setup is not a 3-day experiment; it is a project.
  • Scoring feelings. The output is a decision, not a productivity score.
  • Backfilling observations. If you missed a day, log the miss.
  • Testing five things at once. Then you cannot tell what the evidence is about.

FAQ

Is a 3-day experiment scientific proof?

No. It is a small personal reality check, not a clinical trial or a guarantee. The point is to replace vague intention with a few concrete observations and a decision.

What if I miss a day?

Log the miss. A missed day is evidence too: it may mean the interest is weaker than the intention, or that the action was too large. Do not backfill invented observations.

How do I choose what to test?

Pick one connection that is small enough to act on this week and specific enough to observe. If you have several, test one at a time so the evidence is not mixed.
